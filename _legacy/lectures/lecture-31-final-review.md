## The teacher's chapter walkthrough

### Chapter 10 — Foreign Exchange Market

**Be familiar with all 15 points in the chapter summary (page 307).**

Especially:
- **Purchasing Power Parity** — predicts exchange rates change with relative prices
- **International Fisher Effect** — formula on the exam (REMEMBER: book has wrong S₂ in denominator; use S₁)
- **Law of one price** — connection to PPP
- **Currency swap** — *must mention "with 2 different value dates"* — teacher said this several times, calling it the "important thing to include"
- **Spot vs forward exchange rates** — basics
- **Transaction / translation / economic exposure** — must give EXAMPLES of each
- **Hedging** — protection against FX risk via futures contracts in foreign currencies

> Teacher will "invent some confusing question" of multiple choice; multiple-choice options will look similar. Read carefully, choose precisely.

> **Teacher's interesting observation:** When interest rates are high in a country, IFE says currency should depreciate (high inflation expected). BUT when interest rates are high (like Mexico now), the currency might also APPRECIATE because foreign capital flows in to capture the higher rates. **Two opposite trends — which wins depends on the relative strength of each.** This is "carry trade" overpowering IFE in the short run.

### Chapter 11 — International Monetary System

**Read all of it. Especially:**
- **When did the gold standard cease?** Roosevelt eliminated US gold standard in 1933 (way before Bretton Woods). Final blow was Nixon ending dollar-gold convertibility in **1971**. Floating exchange rates since **1973**.
- **Bretton Woods** system (1944) and its collapse (1973)
- **Floating vs fixed exchange rates** — pros and cons of each
- **Dirty float / managed float** definition
- **IMF vs World Bank** roles — *"must know"*
- **Currency board** — *must mention 100% reserves requirement*
- **Pegged exchange rate** definition

### Chapter 20 — Accounting and Finance in International Business

> Teacher: "I tend to like this chapter because my interests personally involved financial management of the multinational firm."

**Must know:**
- **Lessard-Lorange model** — page 589 — *"Need to know"* — which exchange rate when comparing budget to actual (answer: projected/forward rate, applied to BOTH)
- **Reducing transaction costs** — multilateral netting
- **Minimizing cash balances** — centralized depositories — *"Read or will go bad in the final exam"* — 3 reasons
- **Tax havens** — and the 4 mechanisms to use them
- **4 mechanisms (transfer pricing, royalties, dividends, fronting loans)** — be able to explain in great detail
- **Fronting loan diagram** — page 603
- **Capital budgeting** — page 522 in his version
- **Advantages and disadvantages of transfer prices** — pages 601-602

> **The 4 mechanisms diagram for fronting loan should be drawn cleanly.** Bermuda → London Bank → UK. Bermuda deposits, gets 8% tax-free. UK borrows, pays 9% interest, deducts it.

### Chapter 2 — National Differences in Political, Economic, and Legal Systems

**Be able to explain:**
- **Differences between Anglo (Common law) and Roman/Civil law** — pros and cons of each (don't just say one is better)
- **What are torts?** — including the videos teacher sent (Episodes 1.1 and 1.2)

> **Tort details to know:**
> - Tort = wrongful act other than breach of contract → injury to person/property/dignity/reputation
> - 3 main types: intentional torts, negligence, strict liability
> - Be able to provide an example of each
> - Difference between tort and criminal cases (plaintiff vs prosecutor; liable vs guilty; preponderance vs beyond-reasonable-doubt)

> **Tort reform / why it matters:** The McDonald's hot coffee case (New Mexico) — common law tort outcome very different from how it would have gone in a civil law country like Mexico. Be ready to discuss this comparison.

## Specific tips the teacher gave

### IFE formula — book error reminder
> "Don't correct it now. The book is wrong, class. I repeat, correct your book. It's a typo, an orthographical error. It says S2. You measure the percent change in terms of what it is today, not in terms of what it will be in one year."

Use **S₁ in the denominator**.

### Currency swap definition
> "Important thing needed to include: with 2 different value dates (check book)."

Memorize that exact phrase: "**with 2 different value dates**".

### Currency board
> "What is required to have under a currency board... should be mentioned as part of the answer. 100% reserves."

Always include the 100% foreign currency reserve requirement.

### Consolidation question
> "Determination of the exchange rate gain/loss. Indicate clearly if it's gain or loss."

The label is mandatory. Just the number ($1,310) is not enough. Must say "gain" or "loss".

### Don't ask trivial format questions on the exam
> "Don't ask me 'should I write 1, 2, 3, 4 or A, B, C, D?' These are trivial matters. What does a reasonable person do? It's reasonable to write a thorough complete answer in numerical order, legibly. Don't ask about formatting."

### Wine glass question (Q7 from practice)
> Teacher said: "If you're not going to answer, I probably won't give you the answer now. You should come up with things, OK?"

This is a **strategic / open-ended** question. Multiple valid answers as long as you reason through the trade-offs (hedging vs not hedging, borrowing in yen vs not).

### Floating vs fixed
> "Be prepared to talk about advantages and floating vs fixed interest rates" (he meant exchange rates)

Have at least 2-3 points for each side ready.

### Centralized depositories — teacher's emphasis
> "Read or will go bad in the final exam. You'll lose a bunch of points."

Read pages on global money management in chapter 20. Memorize the 3 reasons for centralization.
