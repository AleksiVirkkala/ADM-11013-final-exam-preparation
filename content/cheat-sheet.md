## Formula reminders

> [!danger] IFE — book is wrong, S₁ in denominator, not S₂
> $$\frac{S_1 - S_2}{S_1} \times 100 = i_D - i_N$$

> [!info] PPP / Big Mac — over/undervaluation
> 1. Theoretical = price_A ÷ price_B
> 2. Ratio = theoretical ÷ actual
> 3. Ratio > 1 → numerator currency **overvalued** by (ratio − 1)
> 4. Ratio < 1 → numerator currency **undervalued** by (1 − ratio)

> [!info] Consolidation — Total decomposition
> $$\text{Operational result} + \text{FX gain/loss} = \text{Total result} \; (= \Delta RE)$$

See [[formulas|full formula sheet]].

## Conversion direction — DON'T mix up

- "X local per 1 USD" (e.g., 17.5 pesos / USD) → **divide** local by rate
- "X USD per 1 local" (e.g., \$1.49 / GBP) → **multiply** local by rate

Teacher said: *"Make this mistake under exam pressure, you lose all 16-21 points."*

## Income statement under Temporal (the tricky bit)

| Row | Rate |
|---|---|
| Sales | Average for year |
| **Cost of sales** | **Historical (when inventory purchased)** ← not current! |
| **Depreciation** | **Same as fixed assets (historical)** ← not average! |
| Other expenses | Average for year |
| Taxes | Average for year |

Under Current Rate, everything in the income statement uses the **average for the year**, including depreciation.

## The 4 tax-reduction mechanisms (memorize)

1. **Transfer pricing** — manipulate inter-subsidiary prices
2. **Royalties** — IP in low-tax sub, charge royalties to high-tax subs
3. **Dividends** — high-tax sub pays dividends to tax-haven owner
4. **Fronting loans** — channel funds via international bank between tax haven and high-tax sub

All four exploit **tax havens**. Be ready to discuss **ethics** (companies legal duty to shareholders vs public/government view as unethical).

See [[topics/tax-mechanisms/index|tax mechanisms]] · [[topics/tax-mechanisms/fronting-loans|fronting loan diagram]].

## 3 FX exposures + example each

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction** | Individual deal FX risk | US airline orders 10 Airbus A330s priced in €; €/$ moves before delivery → cost balloons |
| **Translation** | Reported-statements FX risk (accounting) | Peso depreciation lowers USD value of Mexican subsidiary's equity in parent's books |
| **Economic** | Long-run competitive position | Strong USD makes US exporters uncompetitive globally |

Reduce economic exposure by **dispersing production globally** (real hedge, not financial). See [[topics/fx-exposure/three-exposures|three exposures]].

## Common vs Civil law summary

|  | Common law | Civil law |
|---|---|---|
| **Pro** | Flexible; judges adapt to unique cases | Predictable; cheaper contracts; less adversarial |
| **Con** | Costly contracts (must spell out all contingencies); adversarial | Inflexible; harder to adapt to new situations |

> Teacher dislikes "one is better" stance. Discuss both sides honestly.

## IMF vs World Bank (one-liner)

- **IMF** → maintains **monetary stability** via short-term loans + macro-policy conditions during currency/banking/foreign-debt crises
- **World Bank** → finances **long-term economic development** in poorer countries (IBRD market-rate + IDA 0%-interest arms)

## Gold standard end — three dates

- **1933** — Roosevelt suspended US domestic gold convertibility
- **1971** — Nixon ended USD-gold link
- **1973** — Bretton Woods formally collapsed; floating rates begin

Mention all three for full credit.

## Lessard-Lorange one-liner

Use **Projected (forward) rate for BOTH budget AND actual** when comparing performance. Same rate on both sides eliminates distortion from FX changes during the year.

## Centralized depositories — 3 reasons (must know)

1. Higher interest on larger pooled deposits
2. Better investment expertise in financial hub
3. Smaller required cash buffer (uncorrelated subsidiary needs → variance math reduces precautionary cash)
