> Brief — possible multiple choice. Connects [[international-fisher-effect|IFE]] to short-run reality.

## Carry trade

A speculation strategy:

1. **Borrow** in a low-interest-rate currency
2. **Invest** in a high-interest-rate currency
3. Profit from the rate differential

**Risk:** the high-rate currency might depreciate (canceling the gain — exactly what [[international-fisher-effect|IFE]] predicts in the long run).

In the short run, the carry trade can drive the high-rate currency *up*, opposite to IFE's long-run prediction. Teacher's example: Mexico's interest rates are high but the peso is *strong* right now because foreign capital is flowing in to capture rates → demand for pesos rises → peso appreciates.

**Two opposite trends.** Which wins depends on the relative strength of each. Carry trade tends to dominate in the short run; IFE reasserts in the long run.

## Exchange-rate forecasting — two schools

| School | Belief |
|---|---|
| **Efficient market** | Forward rates are the best possible predictor of future spot rates → don't waste money on forecasting services |
| **Inefficient market** | Markets miss things; forecasting services can do better than forward rates |

### Two methods used by inefficient-market believers

| Method | What it uses |
|---|---|
| **Fundamental analysis** | Money supply, inflation, interest rates, balance of payments |
| **Technical analysis** | Price/volume trends; no economic theory; "compared by many economists to fortune-telling" |

## Currency convertibility (related concept)

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Freely convertible** | Both residents and non-residents can convert any amount |
| **Externally convertible** | Only non-residents can; residents are restricted |
| **Nonconvertible** | Neither can (e.g., old USSR) |

Governments restrict convertibility to preserve foreign exchange reserves and prevent **capital flight** (mass exodus when currency is collapsing).

**Countertrade** = barter-like agreements used when nonconvertibility makes normal payment impossible. Used to be widespread; now <5% of world trade.
