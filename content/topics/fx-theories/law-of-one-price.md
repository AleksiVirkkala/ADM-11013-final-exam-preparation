---
title: Law of One Price
---

> The prerequisite to [[purchasing-power-parity|PPP]]. Q20 on the practice exam asks how they relate.

## Definition

> In efficient markets free of transport costs and trade barriers, identical products sold in different countries must sell for the **same price when expressed in a common currency**.

## Example

A jacket costs \$80 in NYC and £30 in London with exchange rate £1 = \$2.

- Jacket would cost only **£40** in NYC dollars (= \$80 ÷ \$2) vs **£30** at London price
- Arbitrage profit: buy in London for £30, sell in NYC for £40 = £10 per jacket
- Trading flows would equalize prices over time

## Relationship to PPP

| | Law of One Price | PPP |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Single product | "Basket" of goods |
| Implication | Exchange rate must make that one product cost the same | Exchange rate must make the whole basket cost the same |
| Test in real life | Specific goods (oil, gold) | Big Mac Index (uses Big Mac as the basket) |

**PPP is the generalization of the law of one price to a basket of goods.**

## Why neither holds perfectly in practice

- **Transport costs** prevent arbitrage from being free
- **Tariffs and trade barriers** raise prices in some countries
- **Government intervention** in FX markets distorts rates
- **Market power** lets multinationals price-discriminate (McDonald's charges what each market will bear, even for the same Big Mac)
- **Investor psychology** (bandwagon effects) can push exchange rates away from fundamentals in the short run

These imperfections are why Big Mac prices vary across countries even after exchange-rate conversion — and why over/under-valuation calculations have meaning.

## Q20 essay answer template

> *"What is the law of one price? How is it related to the concept of Purchasing Power Parity?"*

**Answer:**

- **Law of one price**: in efficient markets free of trade barriers, identical products must sell for the same price across countries when expressed in a common currency.
- **PPP**: extends law-of-one-price to a "basket" of goods — predicts the exchange rate that would equalize the cost of the basket.
- The **Big Mac Index** is The Economist's PPP test using the Big Mac as the basket.
- In the real world, neither always holds because of transport costs, tariffs, government intervention, market power, and investor psychology — so currencies can be over/undervalued vs PPP predictions.
