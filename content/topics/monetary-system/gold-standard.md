> Teacher's review question: *"When did the gold standard cease to exist?"* The answer depends on which scope. **Mention all three dates for full credit.**

## What it was

> Pegging currencies to gold and **guaranteeing convertibility** into gold at a fixed rate.

Adopted by most major trading nations by **1880**. Each currency had a **gold par value** = the amount of currency needed to buy one ounce of gold.

Example:
- \$1 = 23.22 grains of gold
- 1 oz gold = 480 grains = **\$20.67**
- British pound = 113 grains
- Therefore £1 = \$20.67 ÷ 4.25 = **\$4.87**

## Self-correcting balance-of-trade mechanism

The key strength of the gold standard:

1. A country running a **trade surplus** accumulates gold (foreigners pay in gold)
2. More gold → money supply expands → prices rise (inflation)
3. Higher prices → exports become uncompetitive
4. Trade surplus shrinks back toward zero

And the reverse for deficits. The system naturally balanced trade — no policy intervention needed.

## The three end-dates (memorize all three for the exam)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1914** | Most countries left the gold standard at the start of WWI (war finance required suspending convertibility) |
| 1919-1928 | Major economies returned: US (1919), Great Britain (1925), France (1928) |
| **1933** | **Roosevelt eliminated the US domestic gold standard** during the Great Depression — to expand the economy via monetary stimulus |
| **1944** | Bretton Woods replaced full gold standard with USD-as-anchor system (USD still gold-backed) |
| **1971** | **Nixon ended USD convertibility into gold** — the final blow to any gold linkage |
| **1973** | **Bretton Woods system formally collapsed**; floating exchange rates began |

## How to answer "when did the gold standard cease?"

Three different events count:

| Scope | Date | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| US domestic gold standard | **1933** | Roosevelt suspended convertibility during the Great Depression to enable monetary expansion |
| Dollar-gold link (international) | **1971** | Nixon ended USD convertibility into gold under speculative pressure |
| International fixed-rate system based on gold/USD | **1973** | Bretton Woods collapsed; floating rates began |

> **Tip:** Mention all three dates with brief context. You can't lose points for being thorough.

## Q-style answer

> *"When did the gold standard cease to exist?"*

**Answer:**

The gold standard ceased to exist through three sequential events, depending on scope:

- **1933** — Roosevelt suspended the US domestic gold standard to allow monetary expansion during the Great Depression. Households could no longer redeem dollars for gold.
- **1971** — Nixon ended US dollar convertibility into gold for foreign central banks, under speculative pressure on the dollar. This severed the link between the dollar and gold internationally.
- **1973** — The Bretton Woods system of fixed exchange rates anchored to the dollar formally collapsed, and major currencies began floating. This was the end of the gold-based international monetary system.

In 1976, the **Jamaica Agreement** formalized the floating regime, abandoned gold as a reserve asset, and allowed IMF members to choose their own exchange-rate regimes.
