## One-liner each

| Institution | Core mission |
|---|---|
| **IMF** (International Monetary Fund) | **Maintain monetary stability** — short-term loans + macro-policy oversight during currency, banking, or foreign-debt crises |
| **World Bank** (officially IBRD) | **Promote long-term economic development** — low-interest loans to poorer countries |

Both created at the [[bretton-woods|Bretton Woods]] conference in 1944. Both headquartered in Washington, D.C.

## Full comparison

| | IMF | World Bank (IBRD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Original purpose** | Maintain order in the international monetary system | Finance reconstruction of war-torn Europe (post-WWII) |
| **Today's role** | Lend to countries in financial crises; enforce macroeconomic policy conditions | Long-term low-interest loans for economic development in poorer countries |
| **Loan term** | Short-term + macro policy oversight | Long-term development loans |
| **Funding source** | Member quotas / subscriptions | Bond sales (IBRD) + member subscriptions (IDA) |
| **Two arms** | Standalone | **IBRD** (market rate to risky borrowers) + **IDA** (very low / 0% interest to poorest countries; 50-year repayment) |

## Crisis types the IMF responds to

| Crisis | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Currency crisis** | Speculative attack on currency → sharp depreciation OR forces government to spend reserves & raise rates | Brazil 2002 |
| **Banking crisis** | Loss of confidence in banks → bank runs as depositors withdraw | Iceland 2008 |
| **Foreign debt crisis** | Country can't service its foreign debt obligations | Greece, Ireland, post-2008 |

## IMF policy conditions (controversial)

The IMF typically requires recipients to adopt:

- **Anti-inflationary monetary policy** (raise rates, restrain money supply)
- **Reduced government spending** (austerity)

Critics argue this can cause **short-term contraction** of demand and worsen the crisis (e.g., Greek austerity 2010-2018).

## Moral hazard

Argument that IMF bailouts encourage risky behavior — *"if it goes wrong, the IMF will rescue us, so why not take risks?"* Both governments and lenders may take more risk than they should.

## Q-style essay answer

> *"What are the roles of the IMF and the World Bank?"*

**Answer:**

The **IMF** (International Monetary Fund) and the **World Bank** (officially IBRD — International Bank for Reconstruction and Development) were both created at the 1944 [[bretton-woods|Bretton Woods]] conference but serve different missions.

The **IMF** maintains the stability of the international monetary system. It lends to countries facing acute financial crises — currency crises (speculative attacks causing sharp depreciation), banking crises (bank runs), or foreign debt crises (inability to service external debt). Examples include Brazil 2002, Iceland 2008, Greece post-2008. IMF loans typically come with policy conditions: anti-inflationary monetary policy and reduced government spending. Critics argue these conditions can worsen short-term economic contraction.

The **World Bank** promotes long-term economic development in poorer countries through low-interest loans. Originally aimed at financing post-WWII reconstruction of Europe, today it funds infrastructure, education, health, and poverty-reduction projects in developing countries. The World Bank has two lending arms: **IBRD** lends at market rates to creditworthy developing-country governments, while **IDA** (International Development Association) provides very-low-interest or zero-interest loans to the poorest countries with 50-year repayment terms.

**Simple distinction:** the **IMF maintains monetary stability**; the **World Bank promotes development**.
