> Q18 on practice exam: *"Explain the concept of hedging."* Teacher said "be able to explain in great detail."

## Definition

> **Hedging is protection against foreign exchange risk** by buying futures contracts (forward contracts, currency swaps, options) in foreign currencies.

## Etymology — useful for an in-depth answer

A "hedge" originally was a row of bushes in medieval Europe used to protect property lines. In finance, a hedge is anything that protects against loss.

## Hedge fund ≠ hedging

These are different concepts:

- **Hedging** = protection against FX risk (this page)
- **Hedge fund** = investment vehicle that invests in high-risk securities expecting higher-than-market returns; the "hedge" refers to hedging against losses through portfolio diversification, not against FX risk specifically

Useful distinction to make on an essay.

## Techniques

To hedge **transaction** and **translation** exposures:

1. **Forward exchange contracts** — lock in tomorrow's price today
2. **Currency swaps** — combine spot and forward (see [[spot-forward-swap|currency swap]])
3. **Options** — right but not obligation to convert at a set rate (downside protection without giving up upside)
4. **Lead strategy** — collect receivables EARLY when foreign currency expected to depreciate; pay payables EARLY when it's expected to appreciate. See [[lead-lag|lead and lag]].
5. **Lag strategy** — opposite: delay collection if currency expected to appreciate; delay payment if currency expected to depreciate

> Memory aid: **L**ead = act early to **L**ock-in good rate; **L**ag = wait when currency moves in your favor.

To hedge **economic** exposure → **strategic / operational** hedges (different):

6. **Disperse production** across countries to balance cost-currency vs revenue-currency
7. **Source globally** so input costs don't all sit in one currency
8. **Diversify markets** so revenue doesn't all sit in one currency

See [[three-exposures#3. Economic exposure|economic exposure]] for examples.

## Q18 essay answer

> *"Explain the concept of hedging."*

**Answer:**

Hedging is the practice of **protecting against foreign exchange risk** by buying futures contracts (forward contracts, currency swaps, options) in foreign currencies.

The term comes from medieval Europe, where a "hedge" was a row of bushes protecting property lines. In finance, a hedge is anything that protects against loss.

**A hedge fund is a different concept** — it invests in high-risk securities seeking higher-than-market returns; the "hedge" there refers to hedging against losses through portfolio diversification, not against FX risk.

**Common hedging techniques** for [[three-exposures#1. Transaction exposure|transaction]] and [[three-exposures#2. Translation exposure|translation]] exposures:

1. **Forward exchange contracts** — lock in a future conversion rate today
2. **Currency swaps** — simultaneous spot + forward trades with two different value dates
3. **Options** — right (not obligation) to convert at a set rate
4. **Lead strategy** — collect receivables early in a depreciating currency; pay payables early in an appreciating currency
5. **Lag strategy** — delay collection in an appreciating currency; delay payment in a depreciating currency

[[three-exposures#3. Economic exposure|Economic exposure]] cannot be hedged purely financially — it requires **strategic** moves: dispersing production globally, sourcing across countries, diversifying markets. Toyota with plants worldwide is a classic example.

See [[../../questions/q18-hedging-definition|Q18 page]].

## Example: wine glass scenario (Q7)

> You manufacture wine glasses. In mid-June, you receive an order for 10,000 glasses from Japan. Payment of ¥400,000 is due in mid-December. You expect the yen to rise from \$1=¥130 to \$1=¥100 by December. You can borrow yen at 6% a year. What should you do?

Analysis:
- ¥400,000 at ¥130/\$1 = \$3,077 today
- ¥400,000 at ¥100/\$1 = \$4,000 in December
- Waiting is a **\$923 gain (~30%)** if your forecast is right

Strategy options:

1. **Don't hedge** — accept the upside; you expect the yen to rise
2. **Borrow yen now at 6%**, convert to USD now, repay the yen loan with the customer's payment in December — locks in current rate but loses the upside (a defensive hedge if you're risk-averse)
3. **Forward contract** — sell yen forward at the locked-in 6-month forward rate

Teacher accepts any well-reasoned answer with trade-offs clearly stated. See [[../../questions/q07-wine-glass-hedge|Q7 page]].
