> **Q12 on practice exam.** *"Explain the distinct classes of intellectual property."* Memorize the 3 definitions + durations.

## Definition

> **Intellectual property** = property that is the product of intellectual activity — software, screenplays, music, drug formulas, brand names, etc.

## The three classes (memorize each)

| Class | Definition | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Patent** | Grants the inventor of a new product or process **exclusive rights for a defined period** to manufacture, use, or sell the invention | ~20 years (WTO TRIPS standard) |
| **Copyright** | Exclusive legal rights of **authors, composers, playwrights, artists, publishers** to publish and disperse their work as they see fit | 50 years after author's death (WTO TRIPS minimum) |
| **Trademark** | **Designs and names**, officially registered, by which merchants or manufacturers designate and differentiate their products (e.g., Christian Dior clothes, Starbucks logo) | Indefinite while in use |

## Why IP protection matters

Without IP protection, no firm has incentive to invest in R&D.

- A pharma patent grants ~20-year monopoly → recoups the \$1B+ and 12-year basic research cost
- A media copyright protects the author's economic interest in their work
- A trademark protects brand reputation and consumer trust

## Enforcement varies wildly by country

- **China**: huge piracy rates (77% software piracy in 2011, cost industry \$9.8B). Famous case: Starbucks won a trademark suit against Shanghai Xing Ba Ke in 2006 — signaled shift toward IP enforcement.
- **185 countries** are members of the World Intellectual Property Organization
- **Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property** (1883) — oldest IP treaty, 170+ signatory nations
- **TRIPS** (Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights, 1994, under WTO) — minimum standards: 20-year patents, 50-year copyrights after author's death

## Connection to [[../tax-mechanisms/royalties|royalty tax mechanism]]

IP is the asset that enables the [[../tax-mechanisms/royalties|royalty tax avoidance mechanism]]:

- Place IP ownership in a low-tax country sub
- Other subs pay royalties for using the IP
- Royalty payments are tax-deductible locally → shifts income out of high-tax country

This is why **tech companies (Apple, Microsoft, Google) are uniquely well-positioned** to exploit tax havens via royalties — their IP is portable and high-value.

## Q12 essay answer

> *"Explain the distinct classes of intellectual property."*

**Answer:**

**Intellectual property** is property that arises from intellectual activity — inventions, creative works, brand names, software, and the like. International law recognizes three primary classes:

**1. Patents** — exclusive rights granted to the inventor of a new product or process to manufacture, use, or sell the invention for a defined period. Under the WTO's TRIPS agreement, patents last roughly **20 years**. They are most valuable in pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, and any field where R&D requires huge upfront investment.

**2. Copyrights** — exclusive rights of authors, composers, playwrights, artists, and publishers to publish and distribute their work as they see fit. TRIPS sets a minimum duration of **50 years after the author's death**. Copyrights apply to books, music, films, software, and other creative works.

**3. Trademarks** — registered designs and names by which merchants and manufacturers designate and differentiate their products (e.g., the Christian Dior name, the Starbucks logo, the Apple bitten-apple icon). Trademarks last **indefinitely while the mark is in use** and properly maintained.

**Enforcement varies internationally.** 185 countries belong to the World Intellectual Property Organization. Key treaties include the Paris Convention (1883) for industrial property and TRIPS (1994) for minimum global standards. Enforcement quality differs: developed economies enforce strongly; developing countries historically had high piracy rates (e.g., China at 77% software piracy in 2011), though enforcement is improving over time.

**Why this matters:** without IP protection, firms have no incentive to invest in R&D or brand-building. IP is also the foundation for the **royalty mechanism** of multinational tax avoidance — companies place IP ownership in low-tax jurisdictions (e.g., Microsoft's IP in Ireland) and charge royalties to subsidiaries in high-tax countries, shifting profits via tax-deductible royalty payments.
