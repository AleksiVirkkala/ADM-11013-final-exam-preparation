> **Q15 on practice exam.** Teacher dislikes the "one is better than the other" stance — present both honestly.

## The three legal systems

| System | Basis | Where used | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common law** | Tradition, precedent, custom — judge-made law | UK, US, Canada, Australia, India, most former British colonies | Judges have power to **interpret** law for unique cases; precedents bind future courts; flexible |
| **Civil law** | Detailed written **codes** | Germany, France, Japan, Russia, Mexico, most of continental Europe and Latin America (80+ countries) | Judges only **apply** the code; less adversarial; less flexible |
| **Theocratic law** | Religious teachings | Most widely: Islamic law in Muslim countries (often blended with common or civil law) | Religious foundations; cannot be changed easily |

## Common law in depth

- Evolved in **England** over hundreds of years
- "Tradition" = legal history; "precedent" = past court decisions bind future cases; "custom" = how laws are applied in specific situations
- Judges have considerable **flexibility** to apply the law to unique circumstances
- Each new interpretation creates new precedent
- → **Contracts must be very detailed**, spelling out all contingencies (because the law itself is "ill specified")

## Civil law in depth

- Based on **detailed codes**
- Judges **apply** codes — they don't reinterpret or create new precedents
- → **Contracts are shorter and less specific** (the code already covers most issues)
- Less expensive to draft contracts; less adversarial dispute resolution

## Theocratic (Islamic) law in depth

- Foundation: Koran + Sunnah (sayings of Prophet Muhammad) + writings of Islamic scholars
- Foundations **cannot be changed** (the documents are holy)
- Application is debated by jurists
- Originally **moral**, not commercial, but extends to commerce
- **Example:** prohibition of *interest* (riba) — considered usury, illegal in some Islamic states. Pakistan since 2001 requires Islamic banking methods. ~500 Islamic financial institutions worldwide manage >\$1 trillion

## Pros/cons table (the Q15 answer)

| | Common Law | Civil Law |
|---|---|---|
| **Pro** | Flexible; judges adapt to unique cases; evolves with society; protects against rigid bad outcomes | Predictable; less adversarial; cheaper contracts; clear codes; faster dispute resolution |
| **Con** | Costly contracts (must spell out everything); adversarial disputes; less predictable outcomes | Inflexible; harder to adapt to new situations not in the code; judges have less power to correct unfair outcomes |

## Q15 essay answer

> *"What are the relative advantages and disadvantages of the Civil Law system (codified) and the Common Law system?"*

**Answer:**

Common law (used in the UK, US, Canada, most former British colonies) is based on **tradition, precedent, and custom** — judges have power to interpret the law for unique cases, and each new interpretation creates binding precedent for future courts. Civil law (used in Germany, France, Mexico, most of continental Europe and Latin America) is based on **detailed written codes** — judges only apply the code rather than create new precedent.

**Advantages of Common Law:**

- **Flexibility** — judges can adapt the law to unique cases the legislature didn't anticipate
- **Evolves** with society and changing circumstances
- Protects against rigidly applying bad outcomes when the code's literal reading would be unjust

**Disadvantages of Common Law:**

- **Costly contracts** — because the law is "ill-specified," parties must spell out all contingencies, increasing drafting effort and risk
- **More adversarial** dispute resolution
- **Less predictable** outcomes — different judges may interpret the same precedents differently

**Advantages of Civil Law:**

- **Predictable** outcomes — the code provides clear rules
- **Cheaper contracts** — parties don't have to spell out everything because the code fills the gaps
- **Less adversarial** dispute resolution
- **Faster** in many cases — judges apply the code rather than weighing precedents

**Disadvantages of Civil Law:**

- **Inflexible** — the code may not anticipate new situations (e.g., new technology, novel business arrangements)
- Judges have **less power** to correct unfair outcomes if the code is silent or wrong
- **Slower to evolve** — requires legislative action to update

**Bottom line:** neither is unambiguously better. Common law's flexibility serves jurisdictions with rapidly evolving commerce; civil law's predictability serves jurisdictions that value stability and accessibility for non-specialists. International business operating across both kinds of jurisdictions must adapt contracting and dispute-resolution practices accordingly.

## Comparative example — McDonald's hot coffee case

The teacher mentioned the **McDonald's hot coffee case** (Albuquerque, New Mexico, 1994):

- An elderly woman spilled hot coffee, suffered third-degree burns, sued McDonald's, won millions
- Common law / tort system enabled a large plaintiff-friendly verdict
- In a civil law country (Mexico, France), the outcome would have been very different — typically smaller damages, less plaintiff-friendly procedure
- **Tort reform** debates revolve around whether the US system over-rewards plaintiffs

This is a likely essay direction comparing the two systems' approach to [[torts|tort liability]].
