---
title: Charging Royalties
---

> Mechanism #2 of [[index|the 4 tax-reduction mechanisms]]. Uses intellectual property as the vehicle.

## How it works

**Royalties = payments for the use of patents, copyrights, trademarks, technology, brand names.**

Place IP ownership in a **low-tax-country subsidiary**. Other subsidiaries pay royalties to use the IP. Income shifts from high-tax to low-tax country.

## Example

**Microsoft Ireland** owns the Microsoft brand and patents.

- **Microsoft Mexico** must pay royalties to Microsoft Ireland to use them
- Mexico's tax: **30%**; Ireland's tax: **12.5%**
- The royalty payment is a tax-deductible **expense** in Mexico → lowers Mexican taxable income
- The royalty payment is **income** in Ireland → taxed at 12.5%

Net: income shifts from Mexico (30%) to Ireland (12.5%).

## Why royalties beat [[dividends|dividends]] on tax efficiency

The key advantage:

- **Royalties** are typically **tax-deductible** in the paying country (treated as a business expense, deducted BEFORE local tax)
- **Dividends** are paid AFTER local income tax — so the high-tax country gets its tax bite first

So royalty mechanism is more tax-efficient when the host country has a high tax rate. Companies typically combine both.

## Dependency on [[../legal-systems/intellectual-property|intellectual property]]

This mechanism only works if you have valuable, defensible IP — patents, copyrights, trademarks. That's why tech companies (Apple, Microsoft, Google) are especially well-positioned for this: their IP is portable and high-value.

Without IP that the high-tax subsidiary needs, there's no business rationale for the royalty payment, and tax authorities will challenge it.

## Real-world examples

- **Microsoft**: brand and patents in Ireland → royalties from subsidiaries worldwide
- **Apple**: Double Irish / Dutch Sandwich — Apple Ireland holds IP, Apple Jersey (Channel Islands, 0% tax) receives onward payments
- **Starbucks**: brand IP placed in low-tax jurisdictions
- **Big pharma**: drug patents in low-tax jurisdictions, with subsidiaries paying royalties
