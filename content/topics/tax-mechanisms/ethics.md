title: Ethics of Tax Avoidance
---

> Q10/Q19 specifically asks: *"On ethical grounds, can such actions be justified?"* — the second half of the essay. You must engage with both sides.

## The teacher's framing

> *"The strategies are technically legal but morally questionable. Be ready to discuss both sides honestly. Don't take a strong position without acknowledging the counterargument."*

## Pro side (justification)

1. **Fiduciary duty to shareholders** — corporate boards have a legal/moral obligation to maximize shareholder value. Paying more tax than legally required is, arguably, breach of that duty.
2. **Legal compliance** — these strategies use *legal* mechanisms (transfer pricing within arm's-length rules; legitimate IP licenses; bank-intermediated loans). Companies aren't breaking laws; they're optimizing within them.
3. **Companies aren't charities** — they exist to create value for owners. Tax planning is part of normal business operation.
4. **Regulatory arbitrage is the system** — if governments compete on tax rates (Ireland at 12.5% vs Germany at 30%), companies legitimately respond to those incentives.
5. **Public services don't disappear** — the high-tax country still collects its share of normal payroll, VAT, property tax, etc. Only corporate income tax is shifted.

## Con side (criticism)

1. **Moral vs legal** — just because something is legal doesn't make it ethical. The strategies often "violate at least the spirit of the law" even when within its letter.
2. **Free riders** — companies use the high-tax country's infrastructure (roads, courts, educated workforce, security) but don't fund it proportionally to the economic activity they generate there.
3. **Inequality** — small businesses can't afford these structures and pay full tax. Large multinationals effectively pay far less. This distorts competition.
4. **Tax revenue shortfall** — when governments lose corporate tax, they raise other taxes (often regressive ones like VAT) or cut public services. Both hurt ordinary people.
5. **Public/regulatory pressure** — Mexico's SAT/Hacienda is increasingly auditing multinationals; EU is unhappy with Ireland's tax structure; Biden proposed a global minimum tax (not approved); public scandals (Panama Papers, Paradise Papers) have shifted opinion.
6. **The "race to the bottom"** — if all countries compete on lowering corporate tax, public services everywhere lose. Coordination problem.

## How to structure the essay answer

The teacher dislikes one-sided answers. Recommended structure:

1. **Lead with the pro side** (1-2 sentences): "Companies have a fiduciary duty to shareholders and these strategies are technically legal..."
2. **Pivot with "however" or "on the other hand"**: "...however, the practices raise serious ethical concerns..."
3. **Cover the con side** (2-3 sentences): inequality, infrastructure free-riding, public-revenue loss, "race to the bottom".
4. **Reference current policy** (1 sentence): "Mexico's SAT, the EU, and the proposed global minimum tax under President Biden reflect mounting institutional pushback."
5. **Conclude with balance** (1 sentence): "Reasonable people can disagree; the legality and the morality are not the same question."

## Current policy context

- **Mexico** (SAT/Hacienda) — increasingly auditing multinational tax structures
- **EU** — unhappy with Ireland's tax structure but can't force change (Ireland keeps Apple deals etc.)
- **OECD Pillar Two / Biden's proposal** — global minimum tax (often 15%) to harmonize rates. Not yet adopted, but signal of direction.
- **Mexico's marginal tax rate is high** → Mexico would *benefit* from global harmonization
- Politically difficult — countries like Ireland want to remain competitive
