> Countries with very low or zero corporate income tax. The destinations for profits shifted via the [[index|4 mechanisms]].

## Definition

> A **tax haven** is a country with very low or zero corporate income tax, used by multinationals as a destination for shifted profits.

## Key examples (memorize at least a few)

| Tax haven | Corporate income tax |
|---|---|
| **Bermuda** | **0%** |
| **Bahamas** | **0%** |
| **Cayman Islands** | **0%** |
| **British Virgin Islands** | **0%** |
| **Channel Islands** (Jersey, Guernsey, Sark) | Near-zero (between UK and France) |
| **Ireland** | **12.5%** (major tech hub) |
| **Hong Kong** | 16.5% |
| **Singapore** | Low |
| **Switzerland** | Low |

## How the 4 mechanisms exploit tax havens

| Mechanism | How |
|---|---|
| [[transfer-pricing\|Transfer pricing]] | Set up a haven sub as an intermediary; book profit there |
| [[royalties\|Royalties]] | Place IP in the haven sub; charge other subs for using it |
| [[dividends\|Dividends]] | High-tax sub pays dividends to haven owner |
| [[fronting-loans\|Fronting loans]] | Haven sub deposits at bank; bank lends to high-tax sub |

## Real-world strategies

### Apple — "Double Irish, Dutch Sandwich"

- **Apple Ireland** (12.5% tax) — owns and licenses IP to other Apple subs
- **Apple Jersey** (Channel Islands, 0%) — receives onward profits from Apple Ireland via Netherlands
- The Dutch step ("Dutch Sandwich") uses Netherlands as a pass-through to avoid Irish withholding tax
- Result: Apple accumulated **\$252B+** in cash overseas, paying virtually no tax

### Microsoft

- **Microsoft Ireland** (12.5%) → owns the Microsoft brand and patents
- **Microsoft Mexico** (30%) → pays royalties and dividends to Microsoft Ireland
- Net: profits shift from Mexico to Ireland

### Uber

- Mexican Uber rides invoice to **Amsterdam**, not Mexico City
- Mexican profits channeled through Netherlands → lower tax than Mexico

## Other notable users

Nike, Starbucks, Amazon, Google — all employ similar offshore structures.

## Teacher's anecdote

> "When I visited Bermuda (population ~65,000), I was struck by the number of bank and insurance company offices — all 'shell corporations' or 'phantom corporations' using Bermuda as a paper domicile."

These are the visible footprint of profit-shifting structures.

## Why this is contested

See [[ethics|ethics page]] for the full discussion. In brief:

- Companies argue they have a duty to shareholders to minimize tax legally
- Governments and the public increasingly view it as unethical
- The EU is unhappy with Ireland's tax structure but can't force change
- Mexico's SAT/Hacienda is increasingly auditing multinationals
- President Biden proposed a global minimum tax to harmonize rates (not approved)
