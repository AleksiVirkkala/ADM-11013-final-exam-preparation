title: Transfer Pricing
---

> **Q1 on practice exam** tests this directly as MC. Plus part of the Q10/Q19 essay. Critical: get the direction right.

## What it is

The price at which goods/services are sold **between subsidiaries of the same firm**. Since both sides are owned by the same parent, the parent can manipulate the price to shift profits.

## The direction rule (Q1 MC)

| Goods flow | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Goods sold **INTO** a high-tax country | **Raise** transfer price | Raises that subsidiary's costs → lowers taxable profit there |
| Goods sold **OUT of** a high-tax country | **Lower** transfer price | Lowers revenue recognized there → lowers taxable profit |

**Result:** profit shifts from high-tax country to low-tax country.

> **Q1 example:** MNC exports components to a subsidiary in country X with a high tax rate. To minimize tax, the natural decision is to **raise** transfer prices on the sale into country X (so country X's subsidiary recognizes higher cost → lower profit → less tax). See [[../../questions/q01-transfer-pricing-direction|Q1 walkthrough]].

## Why this works

Transfer pricing exploits the difference in tax rates between countries. The underlying business hasn't changed, but the **accounting allocation** of profit between subsidiaries has — so the firm's overall tax bill drops.

## Worked example: Two-country scenario

Country A produces (40% tax), Country B sells to public (50% tax). Country A sells all output to Country B.

### Without transfer pricing strategy

| | Country A | Country B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales | \$10,000 | \$12,000 |
| Cost of sales | \$8,000 | \$10,000 |
| Profit | \$2,000 | \$2,000 |
| Income tax | \$800 | \$1,000 |
| Net profit | \$1,200 | \$1,000 |

Combined net profit = **\$2,200**

### With transfer pricing strategy (raise A's price to B from \$10K → \$12K)

| | Country A | Country B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales | \$12,000 | \$12,000 |
| Cost of sales | \$8,000 | \$12,000 |
| Profit | \$4,000 | \$0 |
| Income tax | \$1,600 | \$0 |
| Net profit | \$2,400 | \$0 |

Combined net profit = **\$2,400** (+\$200 / +9%)

Country B has zero profit → no Country B income tax. The MNC's overall tax bill drops.

## Worked example: Three-country tax haven

Use a tax-haven "pass-through" subsidiary in the middle:

- Country A (US, 40%) → sells low to Country B (Bermuda, 0%) → sells high to Country C (UK, 50%)
- Country B never touches the product physically; just invoices

| | Country A | Country B (tax haven) | Country C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | \$8,000 → B | \$12,000 → C | \$12,000 → public |
| Cost of sales | \$8,000 | \$8,000 | \$12,000 |
| Profit | \$0 | **\$4,000** | \$0 |
| Tax (40%, 0%, 50%) | \$0 | **\$0** | \$0 |
| Net profit | \$0 | **\$4,000** | \$0 |

All \$4,000 profit sits in Bermuda. **Zero tax paid anywhere.**

## 4 reasons firms manipulate transfer prices (book p. 601)

1. **Reduce tax liability** — shift earnings from high-tax to low-tax country
2. **Move funds out of a country with expected currency devaluation** (FX risk reduction)
3. **Move funds when dividend remittances are blocked** (capital controls workaround)
4. **Reduce import duties** when ad valorem tariffs apply (use low transfer prices to lower declared value)

## Why governments dislike it

- **Arm's-length pricing rules** (e.g., US IRS Section 482) — transfer prices must reflect what unrelated parties would charge in a market. Burden of proof on the company.
- Distorts internal performance measurement (subsidiary profitability stops reflecting management effort, only tax engineering)
- Ethically dubious — "violates at least the spirit of the law"
- Increasing audit scrutiny worldwide (Mexico's SAT/Hacienda, EU tax authorities)

## Discretion matters

The teacher emphasized: in practice, companies **don't** zero-out profits like the worked example — that's "flagrant" and attracts audits. Real companies shave 5-10% off profits in each jurisdiction, not 100%. The IRS / SAT / European tax authorities apply an **arm's-length pricing standard** (Section 482 in US Internal Revenue Code) — transfer prices must be what unrelated firms would charge each other in the market.

## Advantages and disadvantages (book p. 601-602)

### Advantages
- Reduces global tax bill
- Circumvents host-country capital controls
- Hedges against FX devaluation
- Lowers import duties

### Disadvantages
- Government scrutiny / audits / penalties
- Distorts subsidiary performance metrics — managers can't be evaluated fairly
- Ethical pressure / reputation damage
- Some countries (e.g., Mexico) auditing aggressively
- Conflict with arm's-length rules
