---
title: Charging Dividend Remittances
---

> Mechanism #3 of [[index|the 4 tax-reduction mechanisms]]. The **most common** method of moving funds out of a subsidiary, despite not being the most tax-efficient.

## How it works

The subsidiary in the high-tax country pays **dividends** to a parent or sister subsidiary in a **tax haven**.

## Example

Microsoft Mexico is set up so its legal "owner" is Microsoft Ireland (12.5% tax). Microsoft Mexico pays dividends to Microsoft Ireland:

- Income leaves Mexico (30% tax, paid first on the local profit)
- Arrives in Ireland tax-favorably (12.5%)
- Microsoft Ireland is essentially set up as the legal owner of Microsoft Mexico — a tax structure, not an operational reality

## Tax treatment vs [[royalties|royalties]]

The key disadvantage compared to royalties:

- **Dividends** are paid **AFTER** local income tax — Mexico gets its 30% bite on the profit first, then Microsoft Mexico pays the dividend out of after-tax profit
- **Royalties** are paid **BEFORE** local income tax (treated as a deductible expense) — Mexico loses tax revenue on the royalty portion

So if both were available, royalties are more tax-efficient. Companies typically use **both**:

- Royalties to deduct as much as possible BEFORE local tax
- Dividends to move what's left in after-tax form

## Subject to dividend withholding tax

In many countries, dividends paid abroad face a **withholding tax** at the source. Tax treaties between countries often reduce this withholding rate.

## Why it's the most common method anyway

Despite not being the most tax-efficient, dividends are simple, well-understood, and widely accepted by regulators. Royalties require valuable IP. Transfer pricing requires actual intercompany trade. Fronting loans require a banking relationship. Dividends just require ownership — which every parent-subsidiary structure has.

## Real-world example

Apple's offshore strategy involves repatriating dividends from foreign subs to Apple's tax-haven holding entities, then sometimes back to the US (subject to repatriation taxes, which can be deferred).
