---
title: Monetary vs Non-Monetary Items
---

> Critical distinction for the [[temporal-method|Temporal method]]. Under [[current-rate-method|Current Rate]] this distinction doesn't matter — all assets use the current rate.

## Definitions

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Monetary** | Highly liquid, cash equivalents. Value doesn't fluctuate with market — it represents a fixed amount of currency. |
| **Non-monetary** | Not liquid; market value can change. |

## Classification cheat sheet

| Item | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Banks / Cash | **Monetary** | Literally cash |
| Accounts receivable | **Monetary** | Fixed amount someone owes you |
| Inventory | **Non-monetary** | Market value can change |
| Fixed assets (machinery, equipment, buildings) | **Non-monetary** | Physical, market-priced |
| Accumulated depreciation | **Same as fixed assets** | Contra-asset against non-monetary fixed assets |
| Accounts payable | **Monetary** | Fixed amount you owe |
| Long-term debt | **Monetary** | Debt denominated in fixed amount |
| Capital (stockholders' equity) | **Non-monetary**, but always uses **historical** rate | Reflects original investor contribution |
| Retained earnings | Treated differently per method (see below) | Accumulates over time, no single right rate |

## Treatment of retained earnings — depends on method

- Under **[[temporal-method|Temporal]]**: RE is the **plug figure** — forced to balance the balance sheet, not rate-converted.
- Under **[[current-rate-method|Current Rate]]**: RE uses **historical accumulated average** rate.

## Treatment of exchange adjustment

- Under **[[temporal-method|Temporal]]**: **N/A on the balance sheet**. FX effect appears on the **income statement** instead.
- Under **[[current-rate-method|Current Rate]]**: This IS the **plug figure**. Cumulative across all years.

See [[exchange-gain-loss|where the FX gain/loss appears]] for the full why.

## Why the distinction matters under Temporal

Under Temporal method, monetary items use the **current** (year-end) rate, while non-monetary items use the **historical** (acquisition) rate. The mismatch is what creates the FX gain/loss in the first place.

Under Current Rate, every balance-sheet item uses the current rate (except capital), so there's no mismatch on the balance sheet — but the cumulative effect of FX changes still has to go somewhere, which is the **Exchange Adjustment** line.
