## What you're being tested on

Given a foreign subsidiary's balance sheet and income statement in local currency, plus a list of exchange rates and the **functional currency**, you must:

1. [[functional-currency|Choose the method]] (Temporal or Current Rate) based on the functional currency the teacher gives you
2. [[temporal-method|Apply the correct exchange rate]] to each row to convert to home currency (usually USD)
3. **Plug the balancing figure** — under Temporal it's RE; under Current Rate it's the Exchange Adjustment
4. [[exchange-gain-loss|Compute and label]] the exchange rate gain/loss — must write "**gain**" or "**loss**" explicitly
5. Decompose total result into operational result + FX effect

## The big-picture summary

| Functional currency = | Method | Where FX gain/loss appears |
|---|---|---|
| Home (investor) country currency, OR inflationary country | **Temporal** | **Income statement** (this year only) |
| Host (local) country currency | **Current Rate** | **Balance sheet** as "Exchange Adjustment" (cumulative) |

## The total-result decomposition (memorize)

$$\text{Operational result} + \text{FX gain/loss} = \text{Total result} = \Delta RE$$

Where ΔRE = (Retained earnings 31/12) − (Retained earnings 1/1).

## Why this exists (managerial relevance)

A multinational might have great operations but lose all the profit to FX devaluation (or vice versa). The board of directors needs to know which is which to decide:

- Stay in this country or pull out?
- Invest more or contract?
- Hedge our currency exposure?

If you're earning millions in pesos but the peso is collapsing, your operations are profitable in pesos but you're losing money in USD — it's no longer a good investment. Be ready to explain this in essay form.

## Critical pitfalls

> [!warning] Don't get burned by:
> 1. **Multiplication vs division** — "X local per 1 USD" → divide; "X USD per 1 local" → multiply. Mistake = lose all 21 pts.
> 2. **Forgetting to label** — "\$1,310" alone = no credit. Must say "\$1,310 **gain**" or "\$1,310 **loss**".
> 3. **Picking the method yourself** — teacher specifies the functional currency; you derive the method.
> 4. **Converting the plug** — RE (Temporal) and Exchange Adjustment (Current Rate) are plugs, not rate-converted.
> 5. **Cost of sales under Temporal** uses **historical** average for inventory (NOT current rate).
> 6. **Depreciation under Temporal** uses the **same rate as fixed assets** (NOT average for year).
> 7. **Capital always uses historical rate** under both methods — never the current rate.
