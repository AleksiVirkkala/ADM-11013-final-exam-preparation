title: Functional Currency (Choosing the Method)
---

> **The teacher will tell you the functional currency. You decide which method to use.** This is the first step of the [[index|consolidation question]] and worth getting right — it determines everything downstream.

## The rule

| Functional currency = | Method to use |
|---|---|
| **Home (investor) country currency** (e.g., USD for a US multinational) | [[temporal-method\|Temporal method]] |
| **Host (local) country currency** (e.g., Mexican peso for Pepsi Mexico selling in Mexico) | [[current-rate-method\|Current Rate method]] |
| **High inflation / unstable country** (Venezuela, 1980s Mexico, Argentina) | [[temporal-method\|Temporal]] (functional = USD) |
| **Commodity-priced internationally** (silver, gold, oil — priced in USD globally) | [[temporal-method\|Temporal]] (functional = USD) |

## What "functional currency" actually means

The currency of the **primary economic environment** in which the subsidiary operates. Not necessarily the local currency.

## How to recognize each in real-world clues (in case teacher describes instead of states)

| Clue | Functional currency | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Mexican maquiladora exporting to USA, buys US inputs | USD | Temporal |
| Pepsi Mexico selling soft drinks to Mexicans, paying Mexican salaries | Peso | Current Rate |
| Subsidiary in Venezuela (hyperinflation) | USD | Temporal |
| Grupo Peñoles silver mining (silver priced in USD globally) | USD | Temporal |
| Generic Mexican subsidiary with mostly local operations | Peso | Current Rate |
| Subsidiary in 1980s Mexico hyperinflation period | USD | Temporal |

## Why a high-inflation country implies USD functional

In hyperinflation countries, the parent company's home currency becomes the functional reality:

- Pricing decisions are made in USD-equivalents
- People hoard USD-denominated assets
- Local currency is used only for short-term transactions, not value storage

The teacher's anecdote: When he worked at Pepsi Mexico in the 1980s during 150% annual hyperinflation, his division's functional currency was USD — *"Everybody was buying dollars immediately when they got paid. You'd go down to the money exchange with \$5 and buy a washing machine."*

## Why this isn't the firm's choice

Auditors enforce. International rules apply to all publicly-traded companies. The CFO doesn't get to pick whichever method gives better-looking numbers. The method follows from economic reality.

## Practice question Q13 (essay)

> *"How does a firm determine which method it will use for subsidiary financial statement consolidation? Explain the logic of your answer."*

**Answer:**

- It's NOT the firm's choice — the method is determined by the **functional currency**.
- If functional currency = home country currency → [[temporal-method|Temporal method]].
- If functional currency = host country currency → [[current-rate-method|Current Rate method]].
- The logic is that the method should reflect the economic reality of the subsidiary: a subsidiary that operates primarily in local currency (its functional currency) should have its assets translated at the current rate (Current Rate method, less volatile), while a subsidiary that's essentially an extension of the parent (functional currency = home) should have non-monetary items at historical cost (Temporal method, more volatile but more accurate for an extension).
- The CFO can't pick whichever produces better-looking numbers — auditors enforce.
