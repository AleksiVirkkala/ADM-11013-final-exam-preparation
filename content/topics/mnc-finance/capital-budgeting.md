> Mentioned in teacher's walkthrough — could be MC or background for an essay. Same NPV framework as domestic, with three twists.

## The framework

Same as domestic capital budgeting:

1. Estimate expected cash flows
2. Discount at cost of capital
3. Accept project if NPV > 0

The complications are unique to international projects.

## Three complications

### 1. Project cash flows ≠ Parent cash flows

Earnings may be **blocked from repatriation** by:

- Host-government restrictions
- Local withholding taxes
- Mandatory local reinvestment requirements
- Capital controls

**Stockholders only value cash that actually reaches the parent.** So when you discount, use the cash flow expected to reach the parent, not what the subsidiary earns locally.

### 2. Political risk

Likelihood of political change that harms the business. Extreme case: **expropriation** (government seizes the assets).

Two ways to adjust:

| Approach | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Increase the discount rate** | Penalizes early cash flows heavily — controversial because it overweights the near term |
| **Adjust future cash flows downward** | More popular in practice — directly models the risk |

### 3. Economic risk

Mainly **inflation → currency depreciation → falling value of local-currency cash flows** when converted to home currency.

Adjust by:
- Forecasting depreciation in the cash-flow projections (using [[../fx-theories/international-fisher-effect|IFE]] or PPP)
- Building in a margin of safety

## Why this matters managerially

A subsidiary that's profitable in pesos can be unprofitable in USD after political risk + economic risk adjustments. Capital budgeting must use home-currency cash flows that actually reach the parent.

## Q-style answer

> *"What are the complications of capital budgeting for international projects?"*

**Answer:**

International capital budgeting uses the same NPV framework as domestic budgeting — estimate cash flows, discount at cost of capital, accept if NPV > 0. But three complications matter:

1. **Project cash flows ≠ Parent cash flows** — earnings may be blocked from repatriation by host-government restrictions, taxes, or mandatory local reinvestment. Stockholders only value cash that actually reaches the parent, so discount only the parent's expected cash flow, not the subsidiary's local earnings.

2. **Political risk** — likelihood of political change (in the extreme, expropriation) damaging the project. Adjust by either increasing the discount rate (penalizing early cash flows; controversial) or adjusting future cash flows downward (more popular).

3. **Economic risk** — inflation causes currency depreciation, eroding the home-currency value of local-currency cash flows. Forecast depreciation using [[../fx-theories/international-fisher-effect|IFE]] or [[../fx-theories/purchasing-power-parity|PPP]] and adjust the projections accordingly.
