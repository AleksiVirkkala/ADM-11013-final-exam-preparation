## Question

> What is the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA), and what is the "facilitating payments" exception?

(Likely MC or a short answer in a broader essay on legal systems / property rights.)

## Quick answer

**FCPA = US law from the 1970s.**

- **Illegal to bribe a foreign government official** to win or retain business
- **Requires publicly traded companies to keep detailed records** revealing any violations
- Criminal AND civil penalties for violators

### The "facilitating payments" exception

**Small payments to expedite ROUTINE government actions** are NOT considered bribes under the FCPA. Examples:

- Speeding up permit issuance
- Getting vegetables off the dock through customs

**The distinction:** facilitating payments speed up duties the recipient is **already obligated to perform**. Substantive bribes induce officials to do something they would not otherwise do.

> [!warning] **Common MC trap:** the FCPA does NOT outlaw all foreign payments to officials. Facilitating payments are explicitly excepted.

## OECD Anti-Bribery Convention (1997)

34 major economies (most Western nations, **but NOT Russia, India, or China**) committed to making foreign-official bribery a criminal offense. Includes the same facilitating-payments exception.
