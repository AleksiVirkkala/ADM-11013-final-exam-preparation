## Question

> Two countries, Chile and Argentina, produce just one product: beef. Suppose the price of one kilo of beef is **750 Chilean pesos** and in Argentina it is **60 Argentine pesos**.
>
> a. According to the PPP theory, what should the spot exchange rate be for Chilean pesos per Argentine peso?
>
> b. Suppose the price of beef is expected to rise to **830 Chilean pesos** per kilo in Chile and to **75 Argentine pesos** per kilo in Argentina. What should the one-year forward exchange rate be in Chilean pesos / Argentine peso?
>
> c. Given your answers to parts a and b, and given that the current interest rate in **Chile is 7%**, what would you expect the interest rate to be in Argentina?

## Solution

### Part (a) — Today's [[../topics/fx-theories/purchasing-power-parity|PPP]] rate

$$S_1 = \frac{750}{60} = \textbf{12.5 CLP/ARP}$$

### Part (b) — 1-year-ahead PPP rate

$$S_2 = \frac{830}{75} = \textbf{11.07 CLP/ARP}$$

The Chilean peso *strengthens* against the Argentine peso (need fewer Chilean pesos per Argentine peso).

### Part (c) — Apply [[../topics/fx-theories/international-fisher-effect|IFE formula]]

CLP is numerator, ARP is denominator. Chile (numerator) interest rate $i_N$ = 7%. Find Argentina (denominator) $i_D$.

$$\frac{S_1 - S_2}{S_1} = i_D - i_N$$

$$\frac{12.5 - 11.07}{12.5} = i_D - 0.07$$

$$0.1144 = i_D - 0.07$$

$$\boxed{i_D = 0.1844 = 18.44\%}$$

## Answer

| Part | Answer |
|---|---|
| (a) Spot today | **12.5 CLP/ARP** |
| (b) 1-year forward | **11.07 CLP/ARP** |
| (c) Argentina interest rate | **18.44%** |

## Interpretation

The Argentine peso is expected to depreciate against the Chilean peso (1 ARP buys *fewer* CLP in 1 year: 11.07 vs 12.5). Per IFE, this depreciation reflects expected higher inflation in Argentina, which forces Argentine nominal rates to be higher (18.44% vs Chile's 7%).
