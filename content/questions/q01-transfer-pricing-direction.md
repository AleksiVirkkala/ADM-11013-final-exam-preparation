## Question (multiple choice)

> When a multinational corporation with subsidiaries in many nations chooses to export components to an affiliate subsidiary in country X which has a **relatively high income tax rate**, the natural decision would be to:
>
> a. Lower transfer prices on the sale of goods to the subsidiary in country X
> b. **Raise transfer prices on the sale of goods to the subsidiary in country X** ← ✅
> c. Cease exporting goods to the subsidiary in country X
> d. Reduce dividend payments from the subsidiary in country X to headquarters

## Answer: **B**

## Why

Raising transfer prices = raising the cost to the high-tax subsidiary → reducing its taxable profit there. Moves profit OUT of the high-tax country and INTO the (lower-tax) selling country.

| Rule | When |
|---|---|
| **Raise** transfer price | When goods are sold **INTO** a high-tax country (the country's sub recognizes higher cost → lower profit there) |
| **Lower** transfer price | When goods are sold **OUT of** a high-tax country (less revenue recognized there) |

## Why the other options are wrong

- **(a) Lower transfer prices** would do the *opposite* — recognize even more profit in the high-tax country.
- **(c) Cease exporting** would forfeit a real business relationship to avoid tax — typically too extreme.
- **(d) Reduce dividend payments** addresses a different mechanism ([[../topics/tax-mechanisms/dividends|dividend remittances]]) and doesn't change taxable profit in country X.
