## Question

> Under the theory of Purchasing Power Parity as exemplified by the well-known Big Mac Index of *The Economist*, if the average price of a Big Mac in **New York is US\$5.90** while the average price in **Mexico is 67 pesos**, and the **market exchange rate is 19.5 Mexican pesos per US dollar**, the Mexican peso is **undervalued/overvalued**? And by what **percent**?

## Solution

| Step | Computation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Theoretical PPP rate** | Mexican price ÷ US price = 67 ÷ 5.90 | **11.36 MXN/USD** |
| **2. Ratio** | Theoretical ÷ actual market rate = 11.36 ÷ 19.5 | **0.58** |
| **3. Ratio < 1**, so peso is undervalued | 1 − 0.58 | **0.42 = 42%** |

## Answer

> **The Mexican peso is undervalued by 42%.**

## Interpretation

At theoretical PPP rates, you'd need only 11.36 pesos to buy \$1, but in reality you need 19.5 pesos. The peso buys less than fundamentals would predict — it's **undervalued**.

In real terms, this means goods (like a Big Mac) cost less in Mexico than in the US after currency conversion. The Big Mac in Mexico costs only 67 ÷ 19.5 = **\$3.44** at market rates, vs **\$5.90** in NYC.

## Why is the peso undervalued?

Markets discount the peso relative to PPP because of:

- Perceived corruption
- Drug violence
- Less stable institutions

By contrast, the **Norwegian krona is overvalued** (markets pay a premium for stability, oil reserves, low corruption — see [[../topics/fx-theories/purchasing-power-parity|PPP Norway example]]).
