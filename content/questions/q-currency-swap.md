## Question

> Define a currency swap.

Teacher: *"I'll probably put on the exam. Important thing needed to include: **with 2 different value dates**."*

## Answer

> [!danger] **MUST INCLUDE the phrase "with 2 different value dates."**

A **currency swap** is the simultaneous purchase and sale of foreign exchange **with two different value dates** — typically one spot trade today and one forward trade for a later date — combined in a single contract.

It is used to **hedge foreign exchange risk**: a firm that has dollars today but needs yen today AND will receive yen later but want dollars later can lock in both rates today, removing FX uncertainty across the period.

**Two-thirds of all FX transactions are forward** (mostly currency swaps); one-third are spot.

## Example

Apple has \$1M today and needs ¥120M today; in 90 days it will receive ¥120M from Japanese sales and want dollars back.

Apple does a swap:
1. **Spot trade**: \$1M → ¥120M today
2. **90-day forward trade**: ¥120M → $X dollars (locked in at the forward rate)

Two transactions, two value dates, one combined contract.
