title: "Q8 — Mexico Peso Depreciation Strategy"
---

## Question

> You are the CFO of a US firm whose **wholly owned subsidiary in Mexico** manufactures component parts for your US assembly operations. The subsidiary has been **financed by bank borrowings in the United States**. One of your analysts told you that the Mexican peso is in fact **overvalued right now and is expected to depreciate by 9 percent against the dollar** on foreign exchange markets over the next year. **What actions, if any, should you take?**

## Strategic answer — multiple tactics, structure them

This question rewards a structured CFO-style response. Key elements:

### Identify the exposures

- **Mexican operations earn pesos** → on consolidation, the USD-translated revenue will look smaller after peso depreciation ([[../topics/fx-exposure/three-exposures#2. Translation exposure|translation exposure]])
- **Cash earned in pesos** that gets converted to USD will buy fewer dollars ([[../topics/fx-exposure/three-exposures#1. Transaction exposure|transaction exposure]])
- **Long-run competitive position** — if peso depreciation reflects ongoing economic weakness, this matters for [[../topics/fx-exposure/three-exposures#3. Economic exposure|economic exposure]]

### Tactics to consider

**1. Hedge financially**

- **Forward contracts** to sell forecast peso revenue (lock in a USD rate ahead of the depreciation)
- **Currency swaps** to align peso receipts with peso payables
- **Options** if you want downside protection but keep upside

**2. Adjust intra-company financing — refinance in pesos**

Currently the sub is financed by **USD bank borrowings**. With peso depreciation:

- USD debt in the Mexican sub's books gets MORE expensive in peso terms (need more pesos to service it)
- **Shift to peso-denominated debt** — the depreciation will *reduce* the real burden of the debt over time (you pay back depreciated pesos)

**3. [[../topics/fx-exposure/lead-lag|Lead and lag]] strategy**

- **Lead peso receivables**: collect quickly before peso loses value
- **Lag peso payables**: delay paying suppliers (the debt erodes in real terms)

**4. [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/transfer-pricing|Adjust transfer pricing]]**

- Raise transfer prices on components going from Mexico to the US (move profit out of Mexico while the peso is still strong relative to forecast)
- Caution: arm's-length pricing rules apply; can't be flagrant

**5. Strategic moves (for economic exposure)**

- Source from multiple countries (don't rely solely on Mexico)
- Diversify production geography so no single currency dominates costs

## How to structure the answer

A complete answer would walk through:

1. Identify all three exposures the firm faces
2. List the tactics by category (financial hedges, financing changes, operational lead/lag, transfer pricing adjustments, strategic moves)
3. Make a recommendation (typically: hedge financially via forwards in the short term, consider peso-denominated debt restructuring, and use lead/lag for ongoing flows; reserve strategic moves for longer-run decisions)
