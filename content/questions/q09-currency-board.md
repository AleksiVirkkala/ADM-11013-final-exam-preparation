## Question

> What is the purpose of a Currency Board and how does it work?

> [!danger] **Critical:** Must include **"100% reserves"** for full credit.

## Quick answer

A currency board commits a country to converting its domestic currency on demand into a foreign currency at a **fixed exchange rate**. To make this credible, the board holds **foreign-currency reserves of AT LEAST 100% of domestic currency in circulation**.

This eliminates the government's ability to print money beyond reserves, controlling inflation and increasing credibility.

## Key points to hit

1. **Definition**: fixed-rate commitment to convert domestic currency into a foreign anchor
2. **100% reserves** — must mention
3. **Purpose**: eliminate inflation, increase credibility, limit monetary policy abuse
4. **Mechanism**: interest rates adjust automatically when investors flee
5. **Examples**: Hong Kong (since 1983, \$1 = HK\$7.80, weathered 1997 Asian crisis); Argentina (1991-2002, abandoned)
6. **Drawbacks**: government loses monetary policy control; if local inflation > anchor's, currency becomes overvalued
