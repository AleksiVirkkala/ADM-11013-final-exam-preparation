## Question

> What is a dirty float?

(Teacher's review question.)

## Quick answer

> A **dirty float** (or **managed float**) is a regime where a currency **officially floats** but the **central bank intervenes** in the FX market to keep the currency within an undisclosed range.

Don't say just "it floats within a band" — the teacher wants you to mention **government intervention**.

## Example

**China since 2005** — officially manages the yuan against a basket of currencies, but the People's Bank of China intervenes heavily in practice to keep it within a target range.

## How it differs from neighboring regimes

| Regime | Government role |
|---|---|
| Pure float (USD, EUR, GBP) | No intervention; market sets rate |
| **Dirty float** | Market officially sets rate, but **central bank intervenes** when needed |
| [[../topics/monetary-system/pegged-and-dirty-float\|Pegged rate]] | Government fixes the rate explicitly to a reference currency |
| [[../topics/monetary-system/currency-board\|Currency board]] | Government fixes the rate AND holds **100% reserves** to back it mechanically |
