## Question

> What actions can a firm take to minimize its global tax liability? On ethical grounds, can such actions be justified?

(Q19 is the same question in slight variation.)

## Quick answer

Multinationals use **4 mechanisms**, all of which exploit **tax havens** (countries with very low or zero corporate income tax):

1. **Transfer pricing** — manipulate inter-subsidiary prices
2. **Royalties** — IP in tax haven, charge other subs royalties
3. **Dividends** — high-tax sub pays dividends to tax-haven owner
4. **Fronting loans** — channel funds via international bank

**Ethics:** technically legal but morally contested. Present both sides.

## Key points to hit

### The 4 mechanisms (give an example for each)

| # | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/transfer-pricing\|Transfer pricing]] | Raise transfer prices on goods going INTO high-tax country |
| 2 | [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/royalties\|Royalties]] | Microsoft Ireland (12.5%) charges Microsoft Mexico (30%) royalties for IP use |
| 3 | [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/dividends\|Dividends]] | Microsoft Mexico pays dividends to Microsoft Ireland |
| 4 | [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/fronting-loans\|Fronting loans]] | Bermuda sub deposits at London bank; bank lends to UK sub |

All exploit [[../topics/tax-mechanisms/tax-havens|tax havens]] (Bermuda 0%, Cayman 0%, Ireland 12.5%).

### Ethics — present both sides

**Pro (justification):**
- Fiduciary duty to shareholders
- Technically legal
- Companies aren't charities

**Con (criticism):**
- Companies free-ride on infrastructure they don't fund
- Distorts competition (small firms can't afford these structures)
- Tax shortfall hurts public services
- "Race to the bottom" coordination problem
- Mexico's SAT, EU, Biden's proposed global minimum tax all push back

> Teacher prefers balanced answers. Don't take a strong stance without acknowledging the counterargument.
